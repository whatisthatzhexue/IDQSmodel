[MDA_P003] OPERATIONAL HIGHLIGHTS Production Highlights FY2020 was marked by the challenging landscape for our business operations, stemming from several market and supply chain issues posed by the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. For one, our operations in Malaysia were disrupted by the lockdown imposed by the Malaysian Government from mid-March till end-April 2020. While we were granted approvals to maintain operations due to our essential goods category, various other hurdles arose such as disruptions to trucking and supply activities, and slower response times across other supply chain participants. In spite of that, we still managed to sustain our grinding level to near maximum capacity of 250,000 MT in Pasir Gudang and Batam, as we continued to ensure that our cocoa ingredients are readily available to be distributed to meet our clients’ demand. We also managed to register healthy sales of cocoa ingredients in FY2020, albeit at a slightly lower volume from a year ago. The lower demand for chocolate was a result of closure of travel borders and global lockdowns imposed by numerous often-travelled-to countries in Europe.

[MDA_P004]
[MDA_P004] While prices of cocoa butter saw weakness, prices of cocoa solids firmed up as consumers opted for the cheaper alternative compound chocolate products, of which cocoa powder is the main ingredient. Overall, we maintained a commendable EBITDA of RM349.3 million in FY2020, 5.0% higher compared to RM332.8 million in the previous year, largely contributed by the one-off gain of RM27.9 million from the disposal of an associate, Fuji Global Chocolate (M) Sdn Bhd (formerly known as GCB Specialty Chocolates Sdn Bhd). FINANCIAL PERFORMANCE Group revenue in FY2020 grew 25.3% to RM3.7 billion compared to RM2.9 billion in the last year despite the lower sales tonnage, with the growth driven mainly by maiden contribution from the sale of industrial chocolate by SCHOKINAG. GCB’s gross profit improved 5.2% to RM376.0 million from RM357.4 million previously, as a result of increased selling price of cocoa solids and improved yield per tonne. GCB’s pre-tax profit in FY2020 was sustained at RM267.3 million, 0.1% higher versus RM266.9 million the previous year.

RISKS Competition As a medium-sized cocoa player in the market, we face the risk of heightened competition against major multinational companies with wider presence in the market, larger capacities, and better bargaining power. These players tend to have an end-to-end value chain, from producing and trading of cocoa ingredients to branding and distribution of chocolate and cocoa-based products.

[MDA_P010] Despite that, we are constantly enhancing our competitiveness in this industry. In addition, the expected completion of our grinding facility in Cote D’Ivoire in the first quarter of 2022 would allow us to source directly from one of the key origins of premium cocoa beans, and better position us to ride the recovery trend of chocolate demand, as well as support the production requirements of our Europe expansions. Supply Risk With majority of cocoa beans plantations located around the equator, primarily in South America and Africa, climate change is a key risk. Reduced rainfall or prolonged droughts would affect the yield of cocoa beans, resulting in lower supply of cocoa beans. Furthermore, the long shipment duration of cocoa beans from these regions and cost of delivery may affect our competitiveness in the market.

[MDA_P011] Cocoa bean supply from Southeast Asia has also been on a downtrend over the last few years, thus requiring us to diversify our bean sources to other countries in Africa and South America. To manage this risk, we have planned to establish our foothold into Cote D’Ivoire, the largest cocoa bean producing country, via setting up a new cocoa grinding facility there. Site works for the new facility commenced in 2019 and is progressing smoothly. It is targeted for completion by the first quarter of 2022, and upon completion, the plant will enable easier access to a sizeable raw material source and allow us to meet the increasing demand from customers.

[MDA_P012] GUAN CHONG BERHAD Cocoa Bean Price Volatility Risk As a cocoa bean grinder, most of our cost of goods sold consists of purchases of cocoa beans. Therefore, our performance may be affected by not just changes in supply and demand of cocoa beans, but also price volatility that comes with market speculation. Additionally, the introduction of the living income differential (LID) levy in Cote D’Ivoire and Ghana, to improve the income of cocoa farmers, has come into effect in October 2020. The situation was challenging for us, as we navigated the marketplace uncertainties to continue sourcing premium beans to meet clients’ demand, while achieving grinding profitability. These risks are mitigated through securing forward sales contracts of cocoa ingredients, which allow us to hedge the costs of cocoa beans and minimise our exposure to price volatility. Foreign Currency Fluctuation Risk Despite using MYR as our reporting currency, the majority of our dealings are transacted in foreign currencies, namely USD and GBP. To manage the risk of currency fluctuations, the currency used for the purchase of our raw materials will be matched with the currency of export sales.

[MDA_P013]
[MDA_P013] By doing so, our exposure to currency fluctuations is reduced significantly, but we may still be slightly affected by the timing differences between outgoing payment for beans purchases and incoming payment from export sales. New Market Entry Risk The timely expansions into major chocolate consuming markets of Germany and UK in 2020 follow GCB’s venture into Cote D’Ivoire a year ago. This marks the Group’s first aggressive foray to new markets in our corporate history. As with any new geographical venture, this could present a potential risk to the Group due to differences in culture and business processes. For the Germany expansion, GCB has maintained the existing management team of SCHOKINAG to run its day-to-day business, which has allowed a smooth post-acquisition transition. Whereas for the UK expansion, GCB would be able to mitigate such risks by employing experienced and professional personnel to manage the facilities, pursuing integration to the Group’s operations and strategising to optimise efficiency and communication across our various set-ups.

We note that integration will take time, but we are confident of managing this risk by tapping into our past experience when we expanded into the US and Indonesia. OUTLOOK AND GROWTH STRATEGIES The global economic environment is expected to gradually recover amidst the rollout of COVID-19 vaccines. Having said that, the short-term demand for cocoa ingredients remain challenging as most borders remain closed. Additionally, the implementation of LID in October 2020 by the world’s top two largest cocoa producing countries, Cote D’Ivoire and Ghana, has also resulted in uncertainties in the cocoa beans market. While taking into consideration the market conditions, the Group is still cautiously optimistic of its FY2021 prospects, with global demand for chocolate expected to improve in line with the gradual rollout of vaccines and economic recovery.

[MDA_P016] GUAN CHONG BERHAD Our growth strategies will continue to not only sustain our efforts to increase our market share in the international cocoa ingredients market, but also widen our footprint in the global cocoa supply chain. First off, the expansion into Cote D’Ivoire is a key step forward in putting GCB on the map as a major cocoa ingredients player and enhance its market position and competitiveness. We commenced construction of our new cocoa grinding facility in Cote D’Ivoire in 2019. The initial phase of the factory will have a grinding capacity of 60,000 MT per year, bringing GCB’s total grinding capacity to 317,000 MT per year by the time it is completed. This venture enables us to source premium cocoa beans directly, which will not only help to reduce the bean-to-ingredients cycle by at least three months, but also give us better control over the scheduling and supply reliability of quality beans. The other advantages of entering Cote D’Ivoire are the preferential market access to the European market from the African nation, in addition to being in the same time zone.

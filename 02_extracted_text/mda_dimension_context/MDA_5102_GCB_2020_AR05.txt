The Group also posted a 2.2% higher net profit attributable to shareholders of RM222.7 million in FY2020, compared to
RM217.9 million in the previous year. The increase was largely from the one-off gain on disposal of an associate, Fuji Global
Chocolate (M) Sdn Bhd, to industrial chocolate producer Fuji Oil Asia Pte Ltd in February 2020, as the latter exercised a call
option to acquire GCB’s 27.75% stake in the associate. As at 31 December 2020, the Group’s total assets stood at RM2.9 billion, increasing 42.8% from RM2.0 billion previously,
due to higher property, plant and equipment from the expansions into Germany, Cote D’Ivoire, and UK, as well as increased
inventory level and trade receivables.
[MDA_P010]
Despite that, we are constantly enhancing our competitiveness in this industry. By improving operational efficiency, as well
as delivering products of exceptional quality and services to our clientele, we are able to stand out against our peers to
gain additional market share. In addition, the expected completion of our grinding facility in Cote D’Ivoire in the first quarter
of 2022 would allow us to source directly from one of the key origins of premium cocoa beans, and better position us to
ride the recovery trend of chocolate demand, as well as support the production requirements of our Europe expansions. Supply Risk
With majority of cocoa beans plantations located around the equator, primarily in South America and Africa, climate change
is a key risk. Reduced rainfall or prolonged droughts would affect the yield of cocoa beans, resulting in lower supply of
cocoa beans. Furthermore, the long shipment duration of cocoa beans from these regions and cost of delivery may affect our
competitiveness in the market.
[MDA_P014]
As for the Group’s Cote D’Ivoire venture, we aim to create valuable job opportunities for the local communities, at the same
time providing experienced leadership and workforce development through seconding professionals from our Malaysia
and Indonesia operations. We note that integration will take time, but we are confident of managing this risk by tapping into our past experience when
we expanded into the US and Indonesia. OUTLOOK AND GROWTH STRATEGIES
The global economic environment is expected to gradually recover amidst the rollout of COVID-19 vaccines. Having said
that, the short-term demand for cocoa ingredients remain challenging as most borders remain closed. Additionally, the implementation of LID in October 2020 by the world’s top two largest cocoa producing countries, Cote
D’Ivoire and Ghana, has also resulted in uncertainties in the cocoa beans market. While taking into consideration the market conditions, the Group is still cautiously optimistic of its FY2021 prospects, with
global demand for chocolate expected to improve in line with the gradual rollout of vaccines and economic recovery.

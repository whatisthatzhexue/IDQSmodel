As we navigate the strategic landscape
of our operations, it is essential to gain
a comprehensive understanding of
the prevailing conditions in the cocoa
market. This includes factors such as
supply and demand dynamics, price
fluctuations, economic challenges, and
sustainability milestones—all of which
influence our strategic approach in the
ever-evolving cocoa industry. While the Group acknowledges the
external factors beyond our control, we
remain steadfast in our commitment to
navigating these challenges. Our focus
will continue to be on seizing business
opportunities,
meeting
customer
demand, and generating sustainable
profits to support our long-term growth
objectives. Supply and Demand Dynamics
The cocoa industry experienced a continued supply
deficit during the 2023/24 crop year, following similar
shortages in the previous two crop years. This trend
was primarily driven by reduced cocoa production
in origin countries affected by adverse weather
conditions and diseases, leading to a 14.0% decline
in global cocoa grinding compared to the previous
crop year.

[MDA_P002]
However, there has been an increase in
the planting of cocoa seedlings in Indonesia and
certain regions of South America, which may help
improve future cocoa bean supplies. Price Volatility
The New York and London cocoa futures markets
experienced significant volatility in FY2024, reaching
an all-time high exceeding USD12,600 per MT on 18th
December 2024—an increase of 206% compared
to the previous year. This surge in prices has posed
challenges for cocoa grinders, who are facing higher
input costs for raw materials. In an effort to protect
their profit margins, and compounded by a shortage
of cocoa products, the prices of cocoa-derived
products have also risen. Economic Challenges
Global inflationary pressures have led to higher
interest rates, a trend that is likely to persist amid
ongoing uncertainty about the global economic
outlook. This uncertain economic climate may
dampen discretionary spending, as high household
debt-servicing costs limit consumers’ disposable
income.

[MDA_P003]
Côte d’Ivoire Presence Brings Us ‘Close To
Source’ And ‘Close To Market’
Our expansion into Côte d’Ivoire, the world’s largest
cocoa bean supplier, has strategically positioned us
closer to the source of cocoa beans, allowing us to
initiate sustainability projects from the very beginning
of the procurement process. This move enhances
our cost-effectiveness and strengthens our ability
to serve chocolate-consuming countries in Europe
and the UK, where the majority of our customers
are located. Our growing presence in Côte d’Ivoire
will undoubtedly solidify our long-term position as a
global leader in the chocolate supply chain. Economic Recession Concerns
The looming threat of an economic recession
presents additional challenges. The combination
of inflation, rising interest rates, and escalating
commodity prices may contribute to a global
economic slowdown. While the chocolate industry
is
often
viewed
as
recession-resistant—since
consumers tend to indulge in affordable luxuries
during economic downturns—the extent of this
resilience will depend on the broader economic
climate, including the impact of rising living costs,
such as food and housing expenses.

Leveraging Established Fundamentals To
Meet Stakeholder Expectations
To ensure that we not only meet but exceed the
expectations of our diverse stakeholders, we remain
committed to progress in our key focus areas:
profitability, reliability, business stability, and market
share. In our cocoa processing operations across key
grinding countries, we are dedicated to expanding
production capacity, enhancing product quality,
and prioritising cocoa sustainability, reinforcing our
commitment to ESG considerations in response to
the growing interest among our customer base. We
see
significant
growth
potential
through
capacity-driven initiatives, as demonstrated by
our expansions into new regions like Côte d’Ivoire,
Germany, and the UK since 2019. These strategic
moves reflect our ambitious plans for long-term
growth and have unlocked numerous opportunities
to complement and add value to our core business
of producing cocoa-derived food ingredients. A prime example of this is our integration into
industrial chocolate production.

[MDA_P006]
By utilising internally
produced cocoa-derived food ingredients from
Côte d’Ivoire to manufacture industrial chocolate at
our German production facility, we have established
a fully integrated internal supply chain and achieved
valuable cost efficiencies. Moreover, our presence
and production facilities in Europe and the UK enable
us to label our products as “Made in Europe” and
“Made in the UK,” enhancing our ability to penetrate
markets with strong demand for European and UK-
made chocolate. Together, these strategies empower us to not only
meet the requirements of our key customers but also
to address their expectations across four core areas:
building a global supply chain presence, enhancing
cost-effectiveness, increasing ESG considerations,
and embracing greater sustainability practices. Navigating The Surge In
Historic Cocoa Bean Prices
In
FY2024,
cocoa
bean
prices
surged
to
unprecedented levels, driven by a global deficit
resulting from adverse weather conditions and
widespread diseases in origin countries. In response,
we have implemented several strategic measures
to manage the increased costs and ensure the
sustainability of our operations.

[MDA_P007]
To mitigate the impact of rising prices, we have
adjusted the pricing of cocoa products in the
market. At the same time, we are actively managing
our collection processes to ensure timely payments
from customers. Our vigilant approach also involves
closely monitoring supply dynamics in cocoa
origin countries to ensure a steady influx of cocoa
beans to support our production lines. Furthermore,
we maintain proactive communication with our
banking partners to secure sufficient trade and
working capital lines. Amidst
prevailing
market
uncertainties,
our
unwavering commitment remains focused on
maintaining product quality and navigating these
challenges with resilience. Prepared To Mitigate Recession
Impacts And Rising Costs
High interest rates and inflation are beginning
to impact household spending power. While we
anticipate stable demand for cocoa and chocolate
products in the short term, we remain vigilant about
potential challenges, particularly the risks associated
with
a
global
economic
recession. Although
chocolate
is
generally
considered
recession-
resistant, a decline in consumer purchasing power
could still affect demand.

[MDA_P008]
To address these challenges, we are focused on our
core strategies, including maximising the utilisation
of our cocoa grinding facilities, optimising cost
efficiency, and staying agile in response to changes
in the cocoa landscape. We also prioritise strategic,
forward-thinking planning while upholding ethical
and responsible business practices. We continue to monitor and manage operating costs
and potential risks to ensure our competitiveness
while strengthening stability and efficiency across
our supply chain. These efforts will be crucial in
navigating the uncertain economic future. With
these fundamentals in place, we remain committed
to our three core growth strategies—expansion,
sustainability, and innovation—which will enable us
to maximise cost efficiencies and capture a greater
share of the global cocoa-derived ingredients and

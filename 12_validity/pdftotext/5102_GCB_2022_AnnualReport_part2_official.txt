MARKET OVERVIEW

Despite global concerns about the economic
impact of the Russia-Ukraine conflict, the
global demand for chocolate remained
strong, as demonstrated by the increase in
grinding worldwide. According to regional
cocoa associations, total grinding in FY2022
rose to 3.5 million MT from 3.4 million MT in
the previous year.

The reopening of global borders, increased air travel,
and tourism also contributed to the growth in chocolate
consumption in FY2022. The chocolate market is expected
to continue its growth trend, with Euromonitor International
forecasting it to reach a global retail sales value of $128
billion by the end of 2023, with a cumulative average
growth rate of 1.9% in volume over the next three years to
2025.

There are, however, various macroeconomic factors that
created a challenging market environment for GCB in
2022. The European and UK region has experienced rising
energy costs, which caused by the energy crisis as a result
of Russia-Ukraine conflict.

Global inflationary pressure have been causing interest
rate hike. The company foresees that interest rates may
continue to rise due to the uncertain economic outlook.
Simultaneously, the gloomy global outlook may weigh on
discretionary spending as high household debt-servicing
costs restrict the disposable income of customers.

Moreover, the expected economic recession poses other
significant challenges. The combination of inflation, higher
interest rates, and rising commodity prices is likely to result
in a slowdown of the global economy.

While chocolate is considered a recession-resistant industry
as people tend to indulge in small affordable luxuries while
cutting back on other spending, demand will ultimately
still depend on the overall economic environment and
the extent of broader cost-of-living pressures such as
increasing food prices and housing costs.

GROWTH STRATEGIES

GCB’s ascension from a local producer into the fourth largest
cocoa grinding company in the world is the result of our consistent
emphasis on establishing overarching growth strategies that guide
concerted and collective expansion efforts across the Group. These
strategies have evolved over time and reflect our progress within
the industry as well as the shifting landscape of global priorities.
Despite having established ourselves as a key player in the global
cocoa industry, our ambitions for growth remain robust and we will
rely on the following three core growth strategies that leverage on
our competitive advantages to further cement our reputation as an
innovative and responsible industry leader with an ever-expanding
global presence and product range.

INTRODUCTION

and regions around the world, we remain cognisant that
the manner in which we conduct our business will leave a
lasting impact within the communities where we operate.

practices in every aspect of our operational activities and
place ESG considerations as part of our strategic decision-
making process to ensure the Group remains empowered
to mindfully, responsibly and sustainably generate long-
term value for our stakeholders.

Our efforts extend across the ESG spectrum, from ensuring
sustainable financial performance and maintaining
responsible business practices to helping our employees
thrive as part of the GCB family and leaving a positive
impact in our surrounding communities.

We have dedicated ourselves to nurture an environmentally and socially responsible cocoa sector that can be sustained
well into the future, and in which farmers are supported, natural resources are utilised conscientiously, and employees are
empowered in their growth.

SUSTAINABILITY GOVERNANCE

In the pursuit of our robust ambitions, we are committed to ensuring our operations and practices remain fully compliant
with the law and in alignment with the various regulatory requirements of our industry. In each country or region where
we operate, we are dedicated to ensuring complete adherence to the rules that govern our various business dealings,
including all applicable anti-bribery and anti-corruption laws.

Our collective duty as a Group is encapsulated within the Code which comprehensively outlines our expectations of
all our employees, contractors, suppliers and business partners. The Code was specifically crafted to be practical and
applicable to our day-to-day operations and contains non-ambiguous guidelines on what constitutes acceptable and
unacceptable behaviours. It is conscientiously conveyed to all current and new employees, with full adherence expected
from all members of staff, including Board members.

The Code also stipulates the channels through which issues may be raised and includes a whistleblowing process that
empowers employees to confidentially disclose any potential violations without the fear of reprisal. On this note, a
dedicated investigation team has been set up to examine such concerns, with the proper consequences being imposed
should infringements be discovered.

To underscore our commitment to the highest standards of corporate and sustainability governance across all our
operations, a detailed Group-wide framework has been developed based on the following:

• MCCG 2021
• Bursa Malaysia CG Guide
• Bursa Malaysia MMLR
• Bursa Malaysia SR Guide

Furthermore, we apply the principle of “shared growth” to our overall approach to value creation, in that our desire to
achieve business and operational targets must always be effectively balanced with stakeholder expectations, environmental
stewardship and concern for the communities in which we operate. In accordance with this, the following economic,
environmental and social considerations and practices have been embedded at the Group-level to guide our efforts in
responsibly achieving growth and profitability while creating a positive impact on people and our planet.

MATERIALITY MATRIX

Subsequent to our materiality determination process, we refine our appreciation of the relative importance of each
identified topic to both our business operations and our stakeholders via a materiality matrix. In re-evaluating our material
matters in FY2022, additional topics were taken into consideration following our assessment of recommended GRI
disclosures pertaining to the agricultural sector. In light of our expansion into cocoa producing regions, we have expanded
our sustainability reporting scope to meet stakeholder expectations and provide greater transparency within key ESG
contexts.

Having identified 16 sustainability matters during the assessment process, we gauged their significance using the Materiality
Matrix, for which our impacts and performance will be disclosed to the best of our ability in this report. Sustainability risks
are integrated into our enterprise risk management and GCB will routinely assess these risks to ensure appropriate control
measures are in place.

               Sustainable
               Supply Chain

Sustainable procurement is another fundamental area of
focus in which we have greatly expanded our efforts in
tandem with increasing global scrutiny and appreciation
for higher standards of corporate responsibility. We strive
to ensure all our products are sourced responsibly to meet
or surpass the expectations of our customers in line with
our corporate values on environmental responsibility.

At the same time, we believe in doing our part in
cultivating a thriving and sustainable cocoa sector and
have launched a dedicated program that promotes high
standards of responsibility and sustainability within our
supply chain.

Our Sustainable Procurement Policy and Procedure,
and Cocoa Sustainability Program Policy and Procedure
outlines our core procurement principles for selecting
suppliers that maximise long-term value for our
stakeholders while minimizing negative impacts to
society or the environment.

Our GC&B Program outlines our proactive strategies to
support our supply chain in improving farmer income,
protecting the environment, providing responsible cocoa
and benefitting farming communities.

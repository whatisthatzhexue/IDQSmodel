     2. CHANGES IN MATERIAL ACCOUNTING POLICIES

            (a) Global minimum top-up tax

                  The Company has adopted International Tax Reform – Pillar Two Model Rules upon their release on 2 June 2023.
                  The amendments provide a temporary mandatory relief from deferred tax accounting for the top-up tax, which
                  is effective immediately, and require new disclosures about the Pillar Two exposure (Note 18).

                  As the amendments temporarily exempt the Company from accounting for deferred income taxes related to

             As at 31 March 2024, the government of Malaysia (“MY”) has substantively enacted Pillar Two new legislation to

		 Trade receivables (cont’d.)

		 Recognition and measurement of impairment losses

   In managing credit risk of trade receivables, the Company manages its debtors and takes appropriate actions
   (including but not limited to legal actions) to recover long overdue balances. Generally, trade receivables will
   pay within the credit terms. The Company’s debt recovery process is as follows:

   a) After past due the credit terms, the Company will start to initiate a structured debt recovery process which
         is monitored by the sales management team; and

   b) Above 120 days past due, the Company will commence a legal proceeding against the customer.

   The Company’s objective is to seek continual revenue growth while minimising losses incurred due to increased
   credit risk exposure. The Company trades only with recognised and creditworthy third parties. It is the Company’s
   policy that all customers who wish to trade on credit terms are subject to credit verification procedures. In
   addition, receivable balances are monitored on an ongoing basis. For export sales, the Company does not

   offer credit terms without appropriate approval.

   An impairment analysis is performed at each reporting date using a provision matrix to measure Expected Credit
   Losses (“ECLs”). The customers with similar loss patterns are grouped together. The provision rates based on the
   days past due are then applied to these groupings. The calculation reflects the probability-weighted outcome,
   the time value of money and reasonable and supportable information that is available at the reporting date
   about past events, current conditions and forecasts of future economic conditions.

   The provision matrix is derived based on the Company’s historical observed default rates. The historical observed
   default rates are updated at every reporting date.

                		 Market risk is the risk that changes in market prices, such as foreign exchange rates, interest rates and other
                      prices that will affect the Company’s financial position or cash flows.

            31.6.1 Currency risk

                		 The Company is exposed to foreign currency risk on sales, purchases and cash and cash equivalents that are
                      denominated in a currency other than the functional currency of the Company. The currencies giving rise to
                      this risk are primarily U.S Dollar (“USD”), Japanese Yen (“JPY”), Euro (“EUR”) and Singapore Dollar (“SGD”).

                		 Risk management objectives, policies and processes for managing the risk

                		 The Company hedged 97% (2023: 98%) and 87% (2023: 95%) of its foreign currency denominated sales and
                      purchases of raw materials respectively for which firm commitments existed at the reporting date.

                		 The Company uses forward currency contracts to eliminate the currency exposures for which settlement
                      is anticipated more than one month after the Company has entered into a firm commitment for a sale
                      or purchase. The forward currency contracts must be in the same currency as the hedged item. It is the
                      Company’s policy not to enter into forward contracts until a firm commitment is in place. Most of the forward
                      exchange contracts have maturities of less than one year after the end of the reporting period. Where
                      necessary, the forward exchange contracts are rolled over at maturity. The Company does not designate
                      derivatives as hedging instruments under hedge accounting model.

Aligned with our commitment to sustainability, Ajinomoto (Malaysia) Berhad (“AMB” or “the Company”) consistently
addresses environmental, social, and governance (“ESG”) priorities. As a leading seasoning and food manufacturer, AMB
has implemented proactive initiatives and forged strategic partnerships to minimise environmental impact, ensure ethical
practices throughout our organisation, and enhance community welfare.
As we enter for the financial year ended 31 March 2024 (“FYE2024”), we have commenced full operations in our Bandar
Enstek facility, a smart and eco-friendly factory officially certified with a 'SILVER' rating by the Malaysian Green Building
Index (“GBI”). Incorporating advanced technology for automation and digitalisation, this new facility optimises operations
to improve productivity, while providing employees with a conducive work environment which includes recreational
facilities.
Reflecting our consistent pursuit for excellence and continuous improvement, AMB received the World Halal Excellence
Awards 2022 from the Ministry of Investment, Trade, and Industry (“MITI”) and the Halal Development Corporation Berhad
(“HDC”) on 5th July 2023. In recognition of our high standard of food safety measures, we received the Food Safety System
Certification (“FSSC”) 22000 V5.1 Food Safety Management System which endorsed the safety of our food production,
processing, and distribution. We are very proud of these accolades as a responsible food manufacturer.
In the current reporting year, we expanded our existing ESG roadmap, that details our aspirations, goals and targets to be
achieved by 2030. Further to this, we have enhanced our climate reporting by conducting a qualitative scenario analysis
and initiated our Scope 3 greenhouse gas emissions ("GHG") reporting to include emissions from employee commutes.
In FYE2024, AMB collaborated with the Malaysia Recycling Alliances (“MAREA”) to establish an Extended Producer
Responsibility (“EPR”) programme aimed at improving the collection and recycling of post-consumer packaging,
advancing our efforts to reduce environmental impact.
We have strengthened our ESG supplier evaluation process and crafted a supplier assessment roadmap to actively
engage and support AMB’s supply chain in enhancing sustainability practices. Our dedication to community welfare is
evident in the significant increase, by fivefold, of our investment in community programmes, positively impacting over
44,000 beneficiaries during the reporting year.

Defining the Scope and Boundary

Our sustainability statement (the “Statement”) covers the period from 1 April 2023 until 31 March 2024 for FYE2024. The
scope of our sustainability performance reporting includes operations of the corporate office in Technology Park Malaysia,
Kuala Lumpur and our new production plant in Bandar Enstek Halal Hub, Negeri Sembilan as well as our marketing, sales
and distribution activities.
Due to the relocation of our manufacturing plant from Kuchai Lama to Bandar Enstek during for the financial year ended
31 March 2023 (“FYE2023”), the data presented for that year will include information from both plants.

On The Path Towards Sustainability

AMB's sustainability journey has reached several milestones, shaping our direction toward a low-carbon future. We have
made progress in our ESG initiatives, contributing meaningfully to the sustainability agenda and moving forward, we
remain committed to our long-term strategic objectives as we advance along our sustainability journey.

                                                                 2024

                                              Strategic Collaboration & Progressive Advancements
  With the commencement of our fully operational GBI certified smart factory, AMB further reinforces our commitment
  to sustainable manufacturing practices. In tandem with this, AMB conducted another materiality re-assessment
  involving employees in the assessment process. Further initiatives included the establishment of a supplier assessment
  and evaluation roadmap together with an action plan, as well as collaboration with the MAREA for the development
  and implementation of an EPR programme.

                                                                 2023

                                                    Elevating Governance & Greener Operations
  AMB officially relocated our manufacturing operations to Bandar Enstek which introduced an eco-friendly design
  aimed at improving efficiency, productivity and employee engagement in 2023. As part of our decarbonisation
  efforts, AMB integrated a solar photovoltaic system within the new facility, facilitating the adoption of renewable
  energy. We conducted a materiality re-assessment and identified 14 material sustainability matters, along with the
  establishment of ESG key performance indicators to measure our sustainability progress.

                                                                 2022

                                                   Optimising Operations & Embracing Circularity
  In 2022, AMB embraced the digitalisation of our human resource management system which improved the efficiency
  and productivity of employees’ remote working arrangements. Beyond this, AMB transitioned from using poly-material
  plastic to recyclable mono-material plastic for our packaging, contributing to a more sustainable approach for our
  operations.

                                                                 2021

                                                      Fostering Innovation for People and Planet
  The year 2021 signifies the implementation of workplace improvements prompted by operational adjustments
  necessitated by the COVID-19 pandemic, including the introduction of AMB’s Work Style Innovation (“WSI”) initiative,
  offering employees greater flexibility and opportunities for collaboration in their work environment.

                                                                 2020

                                         Empowering Our People & Driving Sustainability Performance
  Recognising the importance of capacity building, AMB placed our focus and invested in tailored training programmes
  aimed at enhancing the skills and productivity of our employees.

                                                            2017 - 2019

                                                         Building Our Sustainability Foundation
  In 2017, AMB embarked on our sustainability journey and published our inaugural sustainability statement. Over the
  years, AMB focused on solidifying our foundation by establishing a sustainable governance framework which guided
  our sustainability initiatives across five key focus areas: Our Environment, Our People, Our Consumer, Fair Operating
  Practices and Our Community.

Effective corporate governance is critical to executing AMB’s strategy in fulfilling our responsibilities to our stakeholders.

strengthened our climate governance by incorporating the Ajinomoto Group ASV policy into the Company’s business

Strategy

In FYE2024, AMB undertook a qualitative scenario analysis to evaluate the potential climate-related effects on our
operations and financial performance. Climate scenario analysis aids in understanding how climate change may affect
business operations and corporate performance over time, by evaluating both transition and physical risks. The analysis
allows organisations to explore various outcomes, underlying assumptions and potential courses of action or events.

Transition risk:

As the economy shifts from carbon-based energy to a low-carbon model, climate-related transition risks emerge from
policy, legal, technological and market changes. These shifts are anticipated to significantly impact sectors and the
overall economy, depending on their nature, pace and emphasis.

Physical risk:

Physical climate risks encompass the potential for more frequent extreme weather events and long-term shifts such
as rising temperatures and sea levels. These hazards pose substantial risks to operations, with the potential to damage
infrastructure, facilities, and disrupt supply chains on a large scale.

The qualitative scenario analysis included benchmarking against local, regional, and global industry peers. Based on
scenarios projecting average global temperature increases of 1.5ºC or 4ºC by 21001 from post-industrial revolution levels,
the effects of climate change on production were analysed over the short, medium, and long term.

Understanding AMB’s Approach

Embracing the imperative to minimise environmental impact while ensuring operational continuity, AMB is committed to
implementing stringent energy efficiency measures and proactive strategies aimed at reducing our carbon footprint and
contribute towards climate-related risks mitigation. The Ajinomoto Headquarter Sustainability Committee established the
“Ajinomoto Group Guideline on Budgeting GHG Emissions and their Financial Impact” which serve as a reference for all
subsidiaries in calculating GHG emissions. The emission conversion factors were determined based on the CO2 emission
“Factor List” corresponding to each country of operation.

		 Energy Efficiency Measures

AMB cultivates an energy-saving culture in our offices by maintaining a centralised air-conditioning system for the
office building with a controlled temperature set at 25°C. This system is automated to regulate usage, ensuring
efficiency throughout the day. We also utilise LED lighting for common areas such as the car park and the pedestrian
bridge.

We implemented hybrid work arrangements, providing employees with the flexibility to work remotely, contributing
to reduced energy consumption at out office facilities. At the production site, we insulated hot water tanks to
reduce heat loss which results in energy savings.

AMB has outlined goals to further reduce our energy consumption. By 2025, we aim to transition fully from diesel-
operated forklifts on our production site to electric chargeable models.

		 Renewable Energy Deployment

   Since operations commenced at our new plant last year, we have consistently utilised our solar photovoltaic (“PV”)
   system which operates at a capacity of 4,170 kWp to use as a power source in our plant and reduce our reliance
   on fossil fuels.

   This year, we began the process of Renewable Energy Certificate (“REC”) issuance for our on-site solar PV system
   via SELCO, a programme introduced by Sustainable Energy Development (“SEDA”) Malaysia. We have registered
   the solar PV system installation with the Energy Commission and are currently in the application process for the REC
   issuance.

   Additionally, we conducted a feasibility study to invest in a Battery Energy Storage System (“BESS”). The BESS stores
   surplus energy generated during times of high production and releases it during low energy production period,
   ensuring full utilisation of the solar energy generated.

   To further enhance our efforts toward green energy transition, we aim to purchase virtual supply of solar energy from
   reliable third parties through the Corporate Green Power Programme (“CGPP”), initiative introduced by the Ministry
   of Energy and Natural Resources.

INTRODUCING MAGMA

Magma Group Berhad builds on the distinguished legacy of Impiana Hotels Berhad, driving a new era of
transformation, growth, and diversification. Incorporated as Bio Osmo Sdn Bhd in 2006 and listed on the Main Market
in 2007, we entered the hospitality sector in 2019, establishing a leading presence in Malaysia’s regional market. Our
strategic rebranding in December 2023 was more than a name change; it symbolised our evolution into a forward-
thinking conglomerate, expanding beyond hospitality to pioneer sustainable success across diverse sectors while
remaining rooted in our core strengths.
OUR BELIEFS
At Magma Group, we believe that true success stems from partnership. By uniting with clients, communities, and
industry allies, we harness collective strengths to deliver exceptional experiences, drive growth, and elevate hospitality
standards.
OUR VISION
We aspire to lead the hospitality industry into a new era defined by innovative, sustainable, and inspiring experiences.
Magma Group aims to set benchmarks through bold initiatives and strategic alliances, proactively adapting to
tomorrow’s opportunities.
OUR MISSION
We are dedicated to creating long-term value through every endeavour, delivering world-class experiences rooted in
collaboration, integrity, and excellence. As we grow, we remain committed to reshaping the industry while ensuring
tangible benefits for our customers, partners, and communities.

   MANAGEMENT DISCUSSION & ANALYSIS

      (Overview of Group Business)

Magma Group Berhad (“.BHNB” or “the Group”) is actively evolving from a traditional hospitality company into an
innovative, experience-focused property and lifestyle group. Anchored in Malaysia’s hotel industry, Magma manages
a portfolio of distinctive assets under the WOLO and Impiana brands, delivering culturally immersive hospitality across
strategic urban and regional locations.

As of the financial year ended 31 December 2024 (“FYE2024”), the Group’s portfolio includes WOLO Kuala Lumpur,
Impiana Hotel Ipoh, Impiana Hotel Senai, and an associate stake in Impiana KLCC Hotel. The full consolidation of
WOLO Kuala Lumpur under Magma Bintang Sdn Bhd has notably enhanced the Group’s hospitality performance,
fueling robust segmental growth.

Magma’s strategic focus on high-impact locations reflects its commitment to delivering exceptional guest experiences.
Through innovative offerings and targeted market positioning, the Group consistently sets industry standards for
service, quality, and operational excellence.

The Group’s foray into real estate leverages its hospitality strengths to deliver elevated living experiences and long-
term value. As a cornerstone of its growth strategy, property development is set to play a significant role in Magma’s
future trajectory.

Looking ahead to 2025, Magma is well-positioned to seize emerging opportunities in the dynamic hospitality
and tourism sectors. With a strong asset base, proven operational excellence, and an unwavering focus on guest
satisfaction, the Group is poised to lead with innovation and sustainable growth.

For the financial year ended 31 December 2024 (“FYE2024”), Magma Group Berhad (“Magma”) underwent notable
shifts in its financial structure, driven by strategic investments, liability management, and operational adjustments.

Assets

Total assets increased modestly by 1.5%, from RM245.21 million to RM248.76 million. This was mainly due to a 9.5%
rise in Property, Plant, and Equipment, which grew from RM111.19 million to RM121.78 million, supported by key
investments such as the RM12.70 million lease acquisition of the WOLO hotel.

Current assets declined from RM93.80 million to RM85.52 million, primarily due to a 71.2% reduction in trade
receivables (from RM22.24 million to RM6.41 million), indicating improved collection efficiency or reduced credit
exposure. Contract assets also fell by 89.7% to RM0.76 million, suggesting lower levels of ongoing or unbilled project
work.

Liabilities

Total liabilities rose by 13.7%, reaching RM159.82 million (FYE2023: RM140.60 million). The increase was largely due
to a RM25.0 million secured loan, backed by land assets in Pangkor Island, used to fund capital expenditure. Contract
liabilities were fully settled, decreasing from RM0.065 million to nil, reflecting completion of project obligations.

Equity

Shareholders’ equity declined by 15%, from RM104.61 million to RM88.94 million, driven by operating losses and
restructuring expenses. This contributed to a significant drop in net assets per share (“NAPS”) from 17.64 sen to 9.19
sen, underscoring the impact on shareholder value during a transitional year.

Summary

FYE2024 marked a year of financial recalibration for Magma, focused on strengthening its asset base while managing
rising liabilities and equity erosion. Despite challenges, the Group’s strategic investments and improved asset
efficiency provide a foundation for recovery. Heading into FYE2025, Magma aims to enhance its financial position by
leveraging opportunities in the hospitality sector and maintaining disciplined financial stewardship.

ENERGY MANAGEMENT

ELECTRICITY CONSUMPTION
Energy management is a cornerstone of Magma Group’s sustainability initiatives, reflecting our commitment to
environmental stewardship. Our electricity needs are primarily sourced from the Tenaga Nasional Berhad (TNB) grid,
with consumption driven by the operational demands of our hotels and corporate offices. By integrating energy-efficient
practices, we aim to reduce our environmental footprint and support Malaysia’s shift toward sustainable energy.
In FY2024, Magma Group recorded electricity consumption of 5,135.9 Megawatt-hours (MWh), representing a 56.78%
increase from FY2023. This rise is attributed to the inclusion of WOLO Kuala Lumpur hotel in our portfolio, coupled
with its high occupancy rates and operational scale. Additionally, the recovery of the hospitality sector, driven by
economic improvement and increased guest demand, contributed to this trend. These factors underscore the Group’s
growth trajectory while highlighting the need for robust energy management strategies.

    CORPORATE GOVERNANCE OVERVIEW STATEMENT

The Board of Directors (“the Board”) of the Company recognizes the importance of upholding high corporate
governance standards to enhance shareholder value while safeguarding the interests of stakeholders. The Board firmly
believes that strong corporate governance practices contribute to the Group’s operational efficiency and strengthen
oversight of its business, management, and operations.

This Corporate Governance Overview Statement (“CG Statement”) provides a summary of the corporate governance
practices adopted by the Company throughout the financial year ended 31 December 2024 (“FYE 2024”). The CG
Statement has been prepared in compliance with the Malaysian Code on Corporate Governance (“MCCG”) and the
Main Market Listing Requirements (“Listing Requirements”) of Bursa Malaysia Securities Berhad (“Bursa Securities”).
It should be read in conjunction with the Company’s Corporate Governance Report for FYE 2024 (“CG Report”),
which details the Company’s application of each MCCG practice and is available on the Company’s website at
www.magma.my.

The Company’s CG Statement is guided by the three (3) key Principles of good corporate practices as set out in the
MCCG namely: -

i. Principle A : Board Leadership and Effectiveness;
ii. Principle B : Effective Audit and Risk Management; and
iii. Principle C : Integrity in Corporate Reporting and Meaningful Relationship with Stakeholders.

The Board assumes overall responsibility for the leadership, oversight, and long-term success of the Group. It
is accountable for setting the strategic direction, upholding corporate governance standards, overseeing business
conduct, and ensuring effective risk management across the Group.

The Board Charter outlines the specific duties and responsibilities of the Board, including a formal schedule of matters
reserved for Board deliberation and those delegated to Management. As part of its governance practices, the Board
deliberates on significant matters, including the Group’s business strategy, acquisitions, divestments, major capital
expenditures, and financial performance reviews.

To discharge its functions effectively, the Board assumes the following responsibilities:

i. Ensuring that the Group’s goals are clearly established and that strategies are in place for achieving them;
ii. Establishing policies for strengthening the performance of the Group including ensuring that the Management

       is proactively seeking to build the business through innovation, initiative, technology and the development of its
       business capital;
iii. Monitoring the performance of the Management;
iv. Deciding on whatever steps are necessary to protect the Group’s financial position and the ability to meet its
       debts and other obligations when they fall due, and ensuring that such steps are taken;
v. Ensuring that the financial statements of the Group and the Company are true and fair and conform with the law;
vi. Ensuring that the Group adheres to high standards of ethics and corporate behaviour; and
vii. Ensuring that the Group has appropriate risk management/regulatory compliance policies in place.

The Board reviews its Charter as and when necessary, to ensure alignment with corporate objectives and compliance
with evolving legal and regulatory frameworks. It also evaluates and approves new policies proposed by Management,
ensuring adherence to legal requirements and best industry practices.

To facilitate effective governance, the Board has delegated specific responsibilities to its Board Committees, namely:

1. Audit and Risk Management Committee (“ARC”)
2. Nomination Committee (“NC”)
3. Remuneration Committee (“RC”)
(Collectively referred to as “the Committees”).

are able to act in the best interests of the Company without undue influence.

       Reports under this policy may include, but are not limited to:

       i. Fraud;
       ii. Corruption, bribery, or blackmail;
       iii. Criminal offenses;
       iv. Miscarriage of justice;
       v. Endangerment of health and safety; and

       The Whistleblowing Policy aims to:

       ii. Encourage ethical behavior by ensuring employees and stakeholders feel safe reporting misconduct without

               fear of retaliation; and
       iii. Act as an early warning mechanism, allowing the Group to detect and address issues promptly before they

               escalate.

www.magma.my. Reports can be submitted directly to the Chairman of ARC, with contact details provided on the

Functionally, the internal auditor reports directly to the ARC. The primary function is to ensure a regular review of the

operate satisfactorily and effectively within the Group. The internal auditor undertakes the internal audit function based

NeedsBridge Advisory Sdn Bhd, an independent firm for the remaining 2 months for the FYE 31 December 2024.

approved internal audit plan for the FYE 31 December 2024.

reviewed by the ARC members and discussed at ARC meetings. Areas of improvement identified and timelines for
the outstanding matters to be resolved were communicated to the Management for their further action. Follow up

recommended corrective actions for improvement.

INTERNAL CONTROL SYSTEM cont’d

The Board has also established an Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption policy (“ABC Policy”) for the Group, guided by the
Guidelines on Adequate Procedures issued pursuant to Section 17A (5) of the Malaysian Anti-Corruption Commission
Act 2009 (“MACC Act”). It is designed to provide a framework governing the general principles and processes on the
giving and receipt of gratification. The primary aim of the ABC Policy is to promote and maintain good governance,
integrity and accountability within the Group and to govern the Group’s interaction with its stakeholders.

Comprehensive guidelines on human resource management are in place to ensure the Group’s ability to operate in an
effective and efficient manner by employing and retaining adequate competent employees with necessary knowledge,
skill and to carry out their duties and responsibilities effectively and efficiently.

Risk assessment is performed by risk owners on an annual basis or when there is a change in internal and/or business
context in accordance with Enterprise Risk Management (“ERM”) Framework per Risk Management Policy. Internal
controls, as risk responses, are formulated and put in place to mitigate risks identified to a level acceptable by the Board.

While the Management has full responsibility in ensuring the effectiveness of internal controls which it establishes, the
Board has the authority to assess the state of internal controls as it deems necessary. In doing so, the Board has the
right to request for information and clarification from the Management as well as to seek inputs from the Committee,
External Auditors, the Internal Auditor and other experts at the expense of the Company. At operational level, clear
reporting lines are established across the Group and operation reports are prepared for dissemination of critical
information to relevant personnel for effective communication throughout the Group and for timely decision making
and execution in pursuit of the business objectives. Matters that require the Board and the Management’s attention are
highlighted for review, deliberation and decision on a timely basis.

RISK MANAGEMENT cont’d

The Board regards risk management as an integral part of the Group’s business operations and has oversight over
this critical area through ARC. ARC, supported by the Internal Auditor, provides an independent assessment of the
effectiveness of the Group’s ERM framework and reports to the Board. The Group’s ERM is consistent with the
ERM Framework and involves systematically identifying, analysing, measuring, monitoring and reporting on the risks
that may affect the achievement of its business objectives. This ERM framework helps to reduce the uncertainties
surrounding the Group’s internal and external environment.

The ERM process is based on the following principles:

z  Consider and manage risks enterprise-wide;

z  Integrate risk management into business activities;

z  Manage risk in accordance with the Risk Management framework;

z  Tailor responses to business circumstances; and

z  Communicate risks and responses to the Management.

All identified risks are displayed on a risk matrix based on their risk ranking to assist the Management in prioritising
their efforts and appropriately managing the different classes of risks. There is no dedicated ERM department,
however, the Management work closely with the respective operational managers to continuously strengthen the
risk management initiatives within the Group so that the Management could respond timely and effectively to the
constantly changing business environment and is thus able to protect and enhance shareholders value.

The Board recognises the importance of effective ERM in enhancing shareholders value while upholding a high
standard of corporate governance. Combining a strong and sustained commitment from the Board and Senior
Management with a clear direction and oversight from all levels of leadership, the Group embraces a holistic risk
management approach to achieve its business targets with minimal surprises.

In the financial year under review, the Board through ARC has reviewed the risk management updates as well as the
progress of compliance status of the internal control and risk management system.

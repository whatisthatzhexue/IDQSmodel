enhancing our position across the dairy value chain and laying the foundation for
growth in Southeast Asia.

      Macroeconomic Challenges

            Description
            The global and domestic market has improved since last year suggesting that headline inflation may climb slightly as
            we continue to see growth in employment and wages. Nevertheless, the global macroeconomic environment remains
            uncertain and volatile due to sluggish and uneven economic outlooks across nations and ongoing geopolitical
            conflicts, leading to instability in commodity, material and logistics costs.

            The IMF’s most recent world economic outlook published in April 2024 has forecasted the world economy to continue
            growing at 3.2 percent during 2024 and 2025, the same pace as in 2023, with the slight increase in growth amongst
            advanced economies expected to be offset by a modest growth contraction amongst emerging markets and
            developing economies over the next two years1.

            Closer to home, RAM Ratings has maintained its projection of 4.5%-5.5% for Malaysia’s GDP growth in 20242,
            compared to 3.6% in 2023, with the economic momentum showing signs of strength and surpassing expectations in
            the early months of 2024. Meanwhile, Australia’s farmgate prices have begun to ease following elevated prices during
            2023, and is anticipated to continue decreasing in 2024 and 20253.

      Transition to a Low-Carbon Economy

            Description
            The World Meteorological Organisation has confirmed that 2023 is the warmest year on record. In light of the
            accelerating climate crisis, the urgency for substantial reductions in greenhouse gas emissions and a faster transition
            to renewable energy sources has never been more critical.

            According to the U.N.’s Food and Agriculture Organization, livestock is responsible for about 30% of global methane
            emissions4. The Conference of the Parties in its 28th year has brought together the dairy industry players to manage
            greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions through setting reduction commitments for methane5.

            At the heart of our business, we are committed to delivering value with less emissions through prioritising sustainability
            within our industry. Leading with examples, our efforts to lower emissions are driving domestic industry dairy players
            to align with the global initiatives whilst focusing on our competitive strengths.

      Health & Wellness

            Description
            Malaysia continues to grapple with health challenges, as highlighted by the World Population Review 2023, which
            identifies our nation as the most obese in Southeast Asia with an obesity rate of 19.7%6. This issue extends to the
            younger population, with the Malaysian Obesity Society reporting that 29.8% of children aged five to 17 are overweight
            (15%) or obese (14.8%)6.
            At the same time, a recent report by Ken Research has confirmed that Malaysian consumers are increasingly
            prioritising their well-being, fuelling demand for fortified milk, yogurt packed with live cultures, and even dairy
            alternatives catering to specific dietary needs8.
            In response, we are contributing to improving children’s nutrition by launching and promoting a growing up milk and
            milk powder made from pure cow’s milk, fortified with essential vitamins and nutrients for healthy development, and
            free from unnecessary ingredients like hidden sugars that exacerbate obesity and malnutrition among Malaysian
            children. Simultaneously, we are expanding our range of healthy and non-dairy products to address evolving customer
            demand.

      Digitalisation & Industry 4.0

            Description
            With the rapid evolution of digital technology, the FMCG and dairy sectors are experiencing significant changes in
            manufacturing, marketing, and consumption.
            Automation at the farm level has become transformative, reducing the need for manual labour and delivering
            substantial improvements in time and cost efficiency, which positively impact our financial performance. Using data
            and analytics in farming provides crucial insights into weather patterns, crop conditions, and animal health, enabling
            more informed and effective decision-making.
            In the downstream segment, the rise of digital sales and marketing channels has significantly lowered distribution
            costs and facilitated connections with a new generation of tech-savvy consumers. The increased pace of digital
            adoption during the COVID-19 pandemic underscores its importance and potential as a primary growth driver for the
            future.
            By adopting and integrating relevant technologies, we enhance our operations and mitigate risks from emerging
            technologies introduced by new and existing market competitors, thereby maintaining our competitive edge in the
            industry.

ENHANCING SUSTAINABILITY THROUGH WASTE-TO-ENERGY PRACTICES

Sustainable farming is essential for the longevity of our operations, the health of our land and the quality of our products.
Since 2014, we have adopted regenerative agricultural practices to minimise waste and responsibly use natural resources.

Building on these efforts, we have identified biogas facilities as a promising sustainability initiative. These facilities convert
waste from our cow herd into clean, renewable biofuel, which can then meet some of our farms’ energy needs. In April
2024, we completed construction and commenced operations of our first biogas plant at our Muadzam Shah farm, which
upon full utilisation is targeted to reduce our diesel fuel usage by 670,000 litres per year, cutting our CO2 emissions by
9,800 tonnes.

EXPANDING OUR PRODUCT PORTFOLIO

Driven by consistent and strategic innovation to meet evolving market expectations and expand across the dairy value
chain, we successfully commercialised 16 new products in FY2024, increasing our total number of SKUs to 192.

A major highlight was the introduction of a powder variant of our successful Farm Fresh Grow growing up milk, originally
launched in FY2023. By creating a powder form of our nutritious milk, free from hidden or unnecessary ingredients, we
have significantly increased the accessibility of Farm Fresh Grow to meet the rising demand for quality growing up milk.

Other key launches during the year included Almond and Oat Barista Milk variants, Natural Farm Yoghurt, 100% Australia
Organic Milk, Chocolate Mint Milk, Riceberry Milk and Cooking Cream.

Looking ahead, we are on track to enter several new product categories in the upcoming financial year. Notably, we are
venturing into the CPG ice cream market, with Taiping plant set to begin production of CPG ice cream in July 2024 with
a capacity of 70,000 pieces a day, increasing to 320,000 pieces a day by October 2024. By 2025, our Enstek plant will
produce over 700,000 pieces daily.

Additionally, we will soon venture into the butter and cultured milk segments, with butter production beginning at our
Taiping plant and cultured milk at our Larkin facility, both scheduled for the second half of 2024.

STATEMENT
      GUIDED BY OUR VISION TO BE A SUSTAINABLE, HONEST AND CONSUMER-CENTRIC FOOD COMPANY,
      WE HAVE DEEPLY INGRAINED ENVIRONMENTAL, SOCIAL AND GOVERNANCE (ESG) PRINCIPLES INTO
      OUR BUSINESS STRATEGY. OUR HOLISTIC APPROACH AIMS TO ENSURE LASTING BENEFITS FOR OUR

    122 Fa r m F r e s h B e r h a d

continuously enhancing our practices to conserve natural resources, reduce greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions and
champion biodiversity, exemplified by our deployment of eco-friendly farming methods and close monitoring of
our carbon footprint.

As a home grown company, we are dedicated to sharing the value we generate with local communities, actively
prioritising locals for job opportunities, empowering our partner farmers with essential skills and knowledge, and
supporting rural or economically disadvantaged micro-entrepreneurs to drive collective progress across the value
chain. This commitment to driving positive social impact is also reflected in the training and educational
programmes we regularly conduct, both for our workforce as well as for the larger community through our youth
outreach and corporate social responsibility (CSR) programmes.

uphold the highest standards of accountability and transparency in our operations, led by a robust framework
that prioritises ethical conduct, regulatory compliance and responsible decision-making.

These collective efforts underscore our belief that the success of our business should go hand in hand with
positive societal and environmental impacts, which ultimately safeguards our ability to deliver sustainable, long-
term benefits to all of our diverse stakeholders.

  SUSTAINABILITY GOVERNANCE

      Our approach to sustainability governance ensures effective oversight and integration of sustainability principles
      throughout our operations. At its core, this framework is built on established principles, structures and reporting
      mechanisms to facilitate the seamless integration of sustainable practices into our day-to-day activities.
      At the apex of our sustainability governance hierarchy sits the Board of Directors (Board), wielding final authority and
      holding ultimate responsibility for our sustainability agenda. Tasked with overseeing the integration of ESG matters into
      our corporate strategy, the Board strives to balance the interests of various stakeholders while fostering responsible
      growth and long-term value creation.
      Assisting the Board in its oversight function is the ARMC, which plays a pivotal role in identifying and mitigating
      sustainability-related risks. Drawing on the insights from the ARMC, the Board conducts annual assessments of critical
      sustainability issues to ensure the alignment and effectiveness of ongoing strategic efforts.
      Upon completion of these assessments, designated Key Senior Management take charge of executing relevant initiatives,
      relying on operational assistance from relevant members of various departments, divisions and operating entities for the
      effective implementation of strategies, before reporting back to the Board on outcomes.
      To maintain momentum and ensure alignment with our sustainability goals and roadmap, our Management teams deliver
      an annual review of the Group’s sustainability actions to the Board, highlighting the progress made within key sustainability
      focus areas.
      Underpinning our sustainability governance structure is a series of policies aimed at fostering responsible business
      conduct and ethical human rights practices across the Group. Accessible for viewing and download on our corporate
      website1, these policies, including our Code of Conduct and Business Ethics (CCBE), Conflict of Interest Policy and Ethical
      Trading Policy, serve as guiding principles for our workforce, business relationships, strategies and processes.
      Supported by the rollout of targeted training modules, we remain dedicated to embedding these policies at every level
      of the Group, ensuring their effective implementation and reinforcing our commitment to sustainable practices.

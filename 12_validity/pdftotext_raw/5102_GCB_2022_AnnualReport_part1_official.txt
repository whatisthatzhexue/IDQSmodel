Introduction
WHO
WE ARE GCB was incorporated in Malaysia as a public limited
company on 22nd March 2004 and listed on the Main
Market of Bursa Malaysia on 8th April 2005.
Our business activities primarily comprise the
manufacturing of cocoa-derived food ingredients and a full
range of chocolates and couvertures. Our cocoa products
include cocoa mass, cocoa butter, cocoa cake and cocoa
powder, while our industrial chocolate products come in
solid or liquid dark, milk or white varieties. Our products
are sold under various trademarks, including FAVORICH, a
fusion of “High in Flavour” and “Rich in Colour” that became
the first brand we established; Carlyle Cocoa, that mostly
supplies cocoa powder to the US market; SCHOKINAG, a
well-established European industrial chocolate brand; and
FAVORICH CHOCOLATE, a brand that exclusively serves
the UK and European markets.
consuming regions. In alignment with this, our production
facility in Côte d'Ivoire, which is the world’s largest cocoa
bean origin country, enables us to be ‘close to source’, while
our chocolate production facilities in the UK and Germany
bring us closer to our customers from key chocolate-
consuming European countries.
To drive continuous growth and advancement, our Group
has invested in expansion and automation projects to
enhance our operations in Germany and Malaysia. Our
initiatives include the addition of new machinery at our
Malaysian production facilities, which has led to an increase
in efficiency and decreased production costs, while our
debottlenecking project at the SCHOKINAG production
facility has bolstered our supply chain and service provision,
whilst enabling us to expand our product variants to include
a range of gourmet products.
OUR HISTORY
The ascension of GCB has been driven by the clear vision
of our leaders and supported by a well-defined plan for
growth, which has seen us chart robust expansion from
our beginnings as a small cocoa trader in the 1980s to
the leading multi-national player in the global cocoa and
chocolate industry that we are today.
Incepted as a cocoa beans processing facility at the town of
Parit Jawa in Muar, Johor in 1985, our first major milestone
came when we built a new production facility at the Pasir
Gudang Industrial Area to expand our processing capacity
to 6,000 MT. Since then, our emphasis on continuous
capacity expansion and an ingrained culture of innovation
has seen us catapult our status into becoming the top
cocoa processor in Asia. Today, we boast a total annual
bean grinding capacity of 337,000 MT and have firmly
established the reputation of our products across both
local and international markets.
We are presently Asia's largest and the world's 4th largest
manufacturer of cocoa-derived food ingredients, and
have geared our business model towards expanding our
presence within cocoa origin countries and key chocolate-

gourmet products.

and mental wellbeing in their working environment, while continuing to support
their personal and professional development and growth.

Produced by roasting and grinding cocoa nibs from

and is complementary to the production of all

CORPORATE PRINCIPAL ACTIVITIES
We are a globally renowned manufacturer of a wide variety
of cocoa-derived food ingredients and chocolates. Our
range of products include cocoa mass, cocoa butter,
cocoa powder and cocoa cake, along with a full suite of
chocolates and couvertures of dark, milk and white in solid
or liquid form.
MARKET ENVIRONMENT
Post COVID-19 market recovery
The Group enjoyed strong sales for cocoa powder during
the COVID-19 pandemic as consumers continued to seek
out chocolate products, especially those in the affordable
range. As the pandemic normalised in FY2022, we noted
an increase in the demand for chocolate products in
tandem with the recovery of the air travel and tourism
sector. At the same time, shipping costs began evening
out as the inflationary pressures of container shortages
and port congestion began to diminish. The overall easing
of logistical challenges in the first half of the year was
beneficial to the Group’s operations and allowed us to catch
up on delayed shipments as customers took advantage of
favourable freight rates in the second quarter of FY2022 to
make call-off orders for deliveries.
Global inflationary pressures raise operating costs
While many governments provided crucial support to help
their citizens survive the COVID-19 pandemic lockdowns,
there was little they could do to stop the rampant inflation.
The cocoa industry faced a steep spike in the cost of raw
materials,packagingmaterials,constructionmaterials,spare
parts and chemicals. The increase in these costs, along with
a sharp rise in the cost of labour, imposed challenges to the
Group in the form of increasing operational costs.
Easing logistical challenges
International freight rates have gradually normalised
since the second half of FY2022 as the congestion and
shortage issues for global containers lessened with
reduced consumer spending power caused by inflation.
This has resulted in a slight recovery in GCB’s operating
margin due to the gradual correction of shipping rates and
the resolution of shipment deferments from the previous
periods.
Rising energy costs
Europe and the UK faced increasing prices for gas and
electricity in FY2022. Russia’s invasion of Ukraine and
the subsequent drop in Moscow’s oil and gas exports to
Europe caused an undersupply, while demand continued
to increase in the latter half of FY2022 as many countries
lifted COVID-19 restrictions.
Spiking interest rates
The response from many central banks to curb inflation is to
increase interest rates, thus reducing overall demand levels
and alleviating the upward pressure on prices. However, the
higher interest rates are likely to hinder economic recovery
from the current gloomy global outlook, while higher
household debt-servicing costs may weigh on discretionary
consumer spending.
Staying mindful of potential challenges
In an environment of chronic inflation and soaring interest
rates, there remain some concerns about a looming
global recession. However, we take some comfort from
the historical performance of chocolate during periods of
economic downturn, which have traditionally been strong
and largely undeterred by market sentiments.
Nevertheless, we remain cognizant that our ambitious
ongoing expansion plans bring risks in as much as they
provide opportunity for growth. While we will certainly
benefit from reduced supply chain risks as we build our
presence in strategic new regions, we simultaneously
recognizethatovercomingdifferencesinmarketconditions,
business processes, societal norms and cultural practices
will require a committed, coordinated and collective effort
from across the Group.

COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE
Côte d'Ivoire expansion brings exciting business prospects
As a leading player in the global cocoa industry, our
biggest competitors have always been major multinational
companies that often possess a wider market reach,
stronger industry influence and greater financial resources
than GCB. At the same time, these multinational entities
usually boast operations that stretch across vertically
integrated supply chains, which reduces their exposure to
risks such as those caused from logistical challenges and
weakened market demand.
These benefits are what makes our new cocoa processing
facility in Côte d'Ivoire, which began commissioning in
September 2022, a true gamechanger for the Group. The
ability to source premium cocoa beans directly from the
origin is expected to transform our operations, giving us
a shorter beans-to-ingredients cycle at our Côte d'Ivoire
production facility, significantly reducing risks within our
supply chain and empowering us with greater control over
the scheduling of our production and shipping activities.
By enabling the direct supply of cocoa-derived food
ingredients to our operations in Germany and the UK, our
newfound presence in Côte d'Ivoire will also boost our cost
efficiencies and give us increased leeway to capture market
share and achieve long-term growth in these important
markets and beyond.
Managing energy costs
The Group undertakes a variety of strategies to manage
and mitigate the negative impact of rising energy costs.
We have locked in around 20% of forward contracts with
energy suppliers in the UK and Germany until March 2023,
and proactively monitor energy usage with the intention of
achieving greater efficiency and lowering operation costs.
In addition, our solar panels projects at our Malaysia, UK
and Côte d'Ivoire production facilities enable us to generate
a renewable source of energy for self-consumption while
reducing negative impacts to the environment. At the
same time, we take potential price hikes into our cost
considerations in order to pass them along the value chain.
Tackling inflation
Global inflation trends resulted in a sharp increase in our
costs for raw materials, packaging materials, construction
materials, spare parts and chemicals. Although the
price fluctuations remain beyond our control, we have
nevertheless adopted a more prudent approach to
consistently diversifying the supply chain, to ensure lower
cost and maintaining quality. While negotiating for lower
costs is the primary purpose of this exercise, we make no
compromise when it comes to the quality of the materials
procured.
Simultaneously, inflation has caused a steep rise in labour
costs. To circumvent this challenge, we have explored
a variety of automation solutions, with our most recent
innovation being the use of automated robotic arms to
complete repetitive tasks such as packaging and palletising
that would normally require significant use of manpower.
Monitoring of interest rate
To mitigate interest rate risks, we minimise our reliance
on borrowings with potentially high interest rates by
maintaining optimal inventory levels of cocoa beans
and finished goods. Nevertheless, our Group possesses
reasonably good access to public and private sources of
funds, which empowers us to explore opportunities to
reduce our exposure to financing risks.

MARKET POSITIONING
At GCB, we believe that the resilience and adaptability to
react to factors of change within the business environment
are extremely crucial. Given the volatility and fluidity of
the market, there is a need to be able to react quickly and
operate efficiently within a dynamic market in order to
maintain profitability and business stability.
Having established a global footprint, our noteworthy
market share provides us with valuable access to a wide
cross-section of customers and buyers. Our Group is able
to further expand the customer base directly with the
strong support of SCHOKINAG and GCBCUK. This amplifies
our ability to harness our positive reputation and build
strong customer relationships across a diverse customer
base, spanning across trading companies and cocoa-
derived food ingredients users such as small independent
customers to multinational manufacturers. From these
strong connections, we will leverage on our reliable brand
reputation in order to build upon existing relationships as
well as to transform them into strategic partnerships. This is
especially important as we are in an industry which has only
a few key customers in niche markets.
Another key benefit of GCB’s market positioning is our
ability to synergise global operations. We are able to
leverage our international presence across the supply
chain to gain competitive advantages, such as building
production facilities in a strategic location or diversifying
our product range to meet market trends. For instance,
we have diversified to produce high quality cocoa powder
with a customised range of colour and fat content in
order to successfully cater to the varying needs of our
customers. With the acquisition of SCHOKINAG, we have
the opportunity of forming a complete supply chain as
we internally supply the required cocoa-derived food
ingredients for the production of industrial chocolate and
gourmet products.
At GCB, we have also unlocked the element of strategic
positioning with our presence in Côte d'Ivoire – the
world’s largest cocoa bean supplying country – as well
as our presence in the European market that is home to
most of the key chocolate-consuming countries. With
the establishment of our cocoa processing plant in Côte
d'Ivoire, the production facility is slated to pump up cost
efficiencies by enabling the direct supply of cocoa-derived
food ingredients to the Group’s operations in Germany and
the UK. The production facilities commenced production in
November 2022 and undertook its maiden export shipment
in February 2023. Currently already running at full capacity,
our expansion in Cote d'Ivoire has significantly boosted the
competitive synergy of our Group.
On an operational scale, GCB holds strongly to the principle
of providing good quality products, which also extends to
the provision of technical support and after sales service
for customers, consistent product innovation derived
through investment in R&D and the application of modern
technologies to meet the evolving quality and sustainability
demands of the industry.

Chairman’s Statement
Thriving Communities
The Group advocates for ethical and conscientious business practices and supports
philanthropic projects and dedicated CSR activities that focus on enhancing the standard of
living and building knowledge-based communities. We also provide assistance during times of
need and safeguard the individuals and communities impacted by our suppliers.
The Group carried out activities such as supporting local community in times of need through
Mercy Malaysia Flood Respond & Recovery donation, as well as nurturing youths in community
through educational programs such as our support for the AIESEC Speak Up Project.
Sustainable Supply Chain
The Group is committed to ensuring that all our products are sourced and produced in a
responsible way that meets or surpasses the expectations of its customers and reflects the
environmental responsibility that we have set for ourselves. In our efforts in adopting ethical
cocoa trading principles, we have established our GC&B Program to show our commitment in
putting sustainability at the core of our business practices.
This program comprises four pillars of sustainability that focus on: -
1. Improving cocoa farmer income;
2. Benefitting farmers and their community;
3. Protecting environment; and
4. Providing responsible cocoa.
By addressing the issues underlying each pillar, we believe this will enable us to achieve our
final goal of delivering 100% traceable and sustainable cocoa ingredients in our direct supply
chain by 2030.
In 2022, the program benefitted 7,816 farmers reached by GAP training programs and
conducted training that promotes IGA to 1,230 farmers.
GCB is committed to practicing good corporate governance to ensure our business practices
are operating in an ethical and transparent manner, with a goal to creating long-term value for
our stakeholders, including customers, employees, shareholders, and the community.

We proactively monitor and manage
our operating costs, and potential
business risks in order to maintain
our competitiveness, while providing
greater stability and efficiency within
our supply chain despite the uncertain

Compliance Risk
We also continue to take a
comprehensive approach towards
maintaining a robust risk management
and internal control framework that
effectively identifies the potential
strategic, financial, operational
and compliance risks which could
undermine our business operations,
while further putting the necessary
controls in place to mitigate such risks.
Additionally, the Board approved
various new group wide policies,
procedures and codes of conduct
to further enhance and align our
governance framework with global
best practices and provide strategic

Our endeavors in pursuit for long-term
growth are guided by the strategic
priorities of Growth & Expansion,
Sustainability and Innovation to drive
our success story.
These three core growth strategies
enable us to fully capitalise on
enhanced cost efficiencies and
capture a greater share of the global
cocoa-derived ingredients and
chocolate market.
Our efforts throughout the years have
indeed been fruitful, as we earned
our title as the world’s fourth largest
cocoa grinder and have cemented our
position as a significant global player
in the chocolate industry.
Since the Group’s public listing in 2005,
we have been growing exponentially
by increasing our grinding capacity,
diversifying our product offerings,
acquiring strategic assets, and
expanding into new countries. Our
production facilities at Delaware and

provide value added services such as
cocoa cake grinding, cocoa butter
and cocoa liquor melting, as well as
cocoa butter deodorising.

the largest chocolate consuming
nations in Europe to achieve the next
level of growth. With our new Côte
d'Ivoire production facility operating,
this will enable us to supply single-
origin cocoa ingredients to support
our industrial chocolate subsidiaries
in Germany and the UK to meet
the growing demand for premium
chocolate products in Europe.
Our presence and production facility
in Europe also helps with our country-
of-origin marketing by empowering

in Europe". The abovementioned
strategies enable us to not only meet
our key customers' requirements
but also cohesively address their
expectations on four fronts: the
global presence supply chain, cost-
effectiveness, ESG, and sustainability.

NAVIGATING AGAINST HEADWINDS
Looking into 2023, it will remain
an uncertain year ahead as global
inflationary pressures are keeping
interest rates high, while geopolitical
risks between Russia and Ukraine,
coupled with US-China tensions in
the South China Sea, are affecting
global commodity prices of energy
resources, metals, food products and
many others. Against this backdrop,
the IMF World Economic Outlook
report published in January 2023 has
forecasted global GDP growth to slow
to 2.9% from 3.4% in 2022.
Similarly, the Global Economic
Prospects report published in January
2023byTheWorldBankalsoforecasted
Malaysia’s GDP growth for 2023 to
moderate to 4.0% from 7.8% in 2022 as
elevated prices and further monetary
policy tightening are expected to hold
back economic activities this year,
especially investment.
The Group will attempt to navigate
ourselves to ensure our operations do
not lose sight of business opportunities
even in these headwinds, so that we
can meet our customers’ demand and
generate sustainable profits to support
GCB’s growth.

WHO WE ARE
Our core businesses focus on manufacturing cocoa-derived food
ingredients and a full range of chocolates and couvertures. Our cocoa
products include cocoa mass, cocoa butter, cocoa cake, and cocoa
powder. Our industrial chocolate offerings come in solid or liquid forms in
dark, milk, or white varieties. We market our products under four distinct
trademarked brands: FAVORICH, our inaugural brand representing ‘High
in Flavour’ and ‘Rich in Colour,’ serving the global market; Carlyle Cocoa, a
cocoa powder brand primarily for the US market; SCHOKINAG, a renowned
European industrial chocolate brand; and FAVORICH CHOCOLATE, which
caters globally with a focus on the UK and European markets.
Currently, we are recognised as Asia’s largest and the world’s 4th largest
manufacturer of cocoa-derived food ingredients. Our business model is
strategically focused on enhancing our presence in cocoa-producing
countries and key chocolate-consuming regions. Our production facility
in Côte d’Ivoire, the world’s largest cocoa bean origin country, allows us to
operate ‘close to source.’ Additionally, our chocolate production facilities
in the UK and Germany strengthen our connections with customers in
major chocolate-consuming European countries.
To drive continuous growth and advancement, our Group has invested
in production improvements efforts by adding press machines to our
grinding facilities in Malaysia, Indonesia, and Côte d’Ivoire in FY2024.
This investment is expected to enhance and increase our production
efficiency, thereby strengthening our supply chain and service provision.
GCB was incorporated
as a public limited
company in Malaysia
on 22nd March 2004
and subsequently listed
on the Main Market
of Bursa Malaysia on
8th April 2005.
We are the leading force in Asia
and the 4th largest globally in the
production of cocoa-based food
ingredients.
Our History
At GCB, our journey has been
meticulously guided by a visionary
leadership and an astutely crafted
growth strategy, propelling us
from our modest beginnings as
a small cocoa trader in the 1980s
to our current status as a premier
multinational entity in the global
cocoa and chocolate sector.
Commencing as a cocoa bean
processing facility in Parit Jawa,
Muar, Johor, in 1985, our trajectory
underwentapivotaltransformation
with the establishment of a
cutting-edge production facility
in the Pasir Gudang Industrial
Area, significantly enhancing our
processing capacity to 6,000 MT.
Our unwavering dedication to
continual capacity augmentation
and the cultivation of a culture of
innovation has elevated us to the
forefront of the industry. Today,
we proudly hold the distinction
of being Asia’s foremost cocoa
processor, with an impressive
annual bean grinding capacity of
335,000 MT and a distinguished
reputation for our products in
both domestic and international
markets.

Our Diverse
Product Range
COCOA MASS
Cocoa mass, also known as cocoa paste or cocoa liquor, is produced
through a meticulous process of roasting and grinding cocoa nibs
to achieve a smooth, fluid state. This ingredient is crucial in crafting
different types of chocolate, particularly dark chocolate.
COCOA BUTTER
Pressing cocoa mass creates cocoa butter, a key intermediary
ingredientusedinavarietyofsectors,includingchocolateproduction,
personal care products, ointments, and pharmaceuticals.
COCOA CAKE
Derived from the pressing of cocoa mass, cocoa cake consists of
solid fragments of cocoa nibs that are subsequently broken down
into smaller pieces. It plays a vital role in industrial grinding and is
essential for producing finely ground cocoa powder.

INTRODUCTION
We offer an extensive range of cocoa-derived ingredients and chocolates, tailored for various applications

COCOA POWDER
Cocoa powder is made by grinding cocoa cakes and is a key
ingredient in chocolate confectioneries, as well as various dessert
recipes and pastes.
LIQUID CHOCOLATE
Our liquid chocolate, rich in flavonoids and antioxidants, is
produced by expertly combining cocoa butter and cocoa mass.
Through rigorous refinement, we achieve remarkable consistency in
delivering liquid chocolate of superior quality.
CHOCOLATE CHIPS & CHUNKS
Ideal for baking, our precisely crafted chocolate chips and chunks
simplify measurements, melt swiftly, and temper perfectly,
enhancing the chocolate melting process and elevating baking
experiences.
CHOCOLATE BEVERAGES
We create a variety of powdered chocolate beverages by carefully
blending cocoa components with sugar, milk powder, and other
ingredients. These beverages are easily mixed with water to provide
a rich and satisfying cocoa flavor.

Sustainable Cocoa Sourcing
We have reinforced our commitment to sustainable cocoa sourcing
by enhancing our due diligence and traceability system. A structured
supply chain management approach has been implemented, covering
risk assessments, supplier evaluations, ethical procurement practices,
and continuous monitoring. In Côte d’Ivoire, significant advancements
have been made, including the adoption of digital traceability tools, the
implementation of supplier assessment protocols, and the strengthening
of partnerships with local farming communities. These efforts not only
align with evolving global sustainability standards but also ensure
responsible practices throughout our entire value chain.
Supply chain traceability: 23% of our direct sourcing network is
traceable to the farm level.
Sustainable cocoa volume: 58,478 MT of cocoa sourced through our
direct sourcing network.
Caring Beyond Cocoa
Our commitment to CSR has led to significant progress in both
community development and environmental sustainability. We have
fostered stronger relationships with local communities and launched
well-being programmes that have had a positive effect on employee
satisfaction. Guided by the three pillars - Thriving Communities, Happy
Employees, and Healthy Planet - our efforts focus on delivering lasting
value throughout our operations. To support these initiatives, we have
allocated over RM158,000 to CSR programmes.
Employee training hours completed: 16,274 hours of professional
development.
Community impact reach: 570 individuals benefiting from our
community development initiatives.
Energy Transition
GCB continues to expand its renewable energy portfolio through strategic
investments in sustainable power solutions. Our cumulative solar
installation of 7.93 MWp and the use of biomass boilers powered by cocoa
shells have successfully reduced our reliance on grid energy. Building on
these accomplishments, we have extended these proven technologies
to our operations in Côte d’Ivoire while exploring the procurement of
Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs) to further support our shift toward
cleaner energy.

Market Environment
Elevated Cocoa Prices
Cocoa market prices soared to record highs,
exceeding USD12,600 per MT on 18th December 2024.
This sharp increase was largely attributed to lower-
than-expected output in major cocoa-producing
nations, driven by unfavourable weather conditions
and the ongoing impact of swollen shoot disease on
crops in Côte d’Ivoire and Ghana. Together, these
two countries contribute over 60% of the world’s
cocoa supply, and their production challenges have
significantly tightened global markets.
Chronic supply shortages in recent years have
driven global cocoa stocks to historic lows, with the
last three crop years ending in deficits as demand
consistently outpaced production. This upward
pressure on prices is expected to persist into the
upcoming season, as production issues in key
regions remain unresolved.
Global Inflationary Pressures
The cocoa industry continues to grapple with
significant inflationary pressures. Since the onset
of the COVID-19 pandemic, costs for packaging
materials, construction supplies, spare parts,
and chemicals have risen sharply. Additionally,
a substantial increase in labor costs has further
escalated operational expenses for our Group,
presenting ongoing challenges to our cost
management efforts.
Rising Interest Rates
In response to rising inflation, many central banks
have implemented interest rate hikes to curb
demand and mitigate upward price pressures.
However, these elevated rates risk hindering global
economic recovery, particularly in an already
uncertain environment. Higher household debt-
servicing costs may further limit discretionary
consumer spending, posing additional challenges
to economic growth.
Regulatory Landscape
The cocoa industry is increasingly shaped
by evolving regulations aimed at promoting
sustainability. These policies address key issues
such as deforestation and human rights, requiring
companies importing commodities like cocoa to
conduct comprehensive environmental and social
due diligence. The European Union Deforestation
Regulation (EUDR), set to take effect in FY2026, will
mandate that all cocoa products entering the EU
adhere to its stringent standards.
Geopolitical Tension
The Red Sea crisis is expected to disrupt the cocoa
trade as shipping companies suspend or reroute
their shipments of traded goods. This has resulted
in soaring shipping costs, driven by extended transit
times, rising expenses, and heightened insurance
risk premiums. With cocoa prices already at record
highs, the added strain of increased freight rates
is likely to intensify pricing pressures across the
industry.
Challenges on the Horizon
In an environment marked by persistent inflation
and rising interest rates, concerns about a potential
global recession remain prominent. Yet, historical
data shows that chocolate consumption has proven
resilient even during economic downturns.
Despite this resilience, we approach our ambitious
expansion plans with caution. Entering new regions
offers significant growth opportunities and helps
mitigate supply chain risks, but it also requires a
coordinated and collective effort across the Group.
Such an approach is critical to effectively navigate
the diverse market conditions, business practices,
societal norms, and cultural nuances present in
each region.

Competitive Landscape
Côte d’Ivoire Expansion
Establishing operations in the world’s largest cocoa bean-producing country positions us strategically for
a competitive edge. Our cocoa processing facility in Côte d’Ivoire, commissioned in September 2022 and
inaugurated in July 2023 by the country’s vice president, became fully operational in November 2022. It
completed its maiden export shipment in February 2023, marking a significant milestone for our business. With
a potential capacity of 240,000 MT, we have been proactively exploring ways to optimise capacity utilisation
and unlock its full potential since its commissioning.
Equipped with cutting-edge machinery and the latest technology, the facility allows us to source premium
cocoa beans directly from the origin, significantly reducing the beans-to-ingredients cycle. This strategic
expansion also enhances our supply chain resilience, providing greater control over production and shipping
schedules. By supplying cocoa-derived ingredients directly to our operations in Germany and the UK, we
anticipate improved cost efficiencies, strengthening our competitive position in these critical markets and
beyond.
Looking ahead, we are actively exploring further opportunities to expand the processing capacity at our Côte
d’Ivoire facility, positioning us to tap into additional strategic consumer markets.

MARKET
OVERVIEW
OUTLOOK
As we navigate the strategic landscape
of our operations, it is essential to gain
a comprehensive understanding of
the prevailing conditions in the cocoa
market. This includes factors such as
supply and demand dynamics, price
fluctuations, economic challenges, and
sustainability milestones—all of which
influence our strategic approach in the
ever-evolving cocoa industry.
While the Group acknowledges the
external factors beyond our control, we
remain steadfast in our commitment to
navigating these challenges. Our focus
will continue to be on seizing business
opportunities, meeting customer
demand, and generating sustainable
profits to support our long-term growth
objectives.
Supply and Demand Dynamics
The cocoa industry experienced a continued supply
deficit during the 2023/24 crop year, following similar
shortages in the previous two crop years. This trend
was primarily driven by reduced cocoa production
in origin countries affected by adverse weather
conditions and diseases, leading to a 14.0% decline
in global cocoa grinding compared to the previous
crop year. However, there has been an increase in
the planting of cocoa seedlings in Indonesia and
certain regions of South America, which may help
improve future cocoa bean supplies.
Price Volatility
The New York and London cocoa futures markets
experienced significant volatility in FY2024, reaching
an all-time high exceeding USD12,600 per MT on 18th
December 2024—an increase of 206% compared
to the previous year. This surge in prices has posed
challenges for cocoa grinders, who are facing higher
input costs for raw materials. In an effort to protect
their profit margins, and compounded by a shortage
of cocoa products, the prices of cocoa-derived
products have also risen.
Economic Challenges
Global inflationary pressures have led to higher
interest rates, a trend that is likely to persist amid
ongoing uncertainty about the global economic
outlook. This uncertain economic climate may
dampen discretionary spending, as high household
debt-servicing costs limit consumers’ disposable
income.
Côte d’Ivoire Presence Brings Us ‘Close To
Source’ And ‘Close To Market’
Our expansion into Côte d’Ivoire, the world’s largest
cocoa bean supplier, has strategically positioned us
closer to the source of cocoa beans, allowing us to
initiate sustainability projects from the very beginning
of the procurement process. This move enhances
our cost-effectiveness and strengthens our ability
to serve chocolate-consuming countries in Europe
and the UK, where the majority of our customers
are located. Our growing presence in Côte d’Ivoire
will undoubtedly solidify our long-term position as a
global leader in the chocolate supply chain.
Economic Recession Concerns
The looming threat of an economic recession
presents additional challenges. The combination
of inflation, rising interest rates, and escalating
commodity prices may contribute to a global
economic slowdown. While the chocolate industry
is often viewed as recession-resistant—since
consumers tend to indulge in affordable luxuries
during economic downturns—the extent of this
resilience will depend on the broader economic
climate, including the impact of rising living costs,
such as food and housing expenses.
Sustainability Advancements
FY2024 marked a significant milestone in
sustainability, with the EU formalising regulations
on deforestation-free products. In response,
industry stakeholders are collaborating to ensure
comprehensive traceability throughout the cocoa
value chain, reinforcing their commitment to
sustainable practices. This focus on sustainability
is essential in offering consumers transparency
about the origin and production methods of cocoa
products, ultimately enhancing the credibility and
long-term sustainability of the entire industry.

Leveraging Established Fundamentals To
Meet Stakeholder Expectations
To ensure that we not only meet but exceed the
expectations of our diverse stakeholders, we remain
committed to progress in our key focus areas:
profitability, reliability, business stability, and market
share. In our cocoa processing operations across key
grinding countries, we are dedicated to expanding
production capacity, enhancing product quality,
and prioritising cocoa sustainability, reinforcing our
commitment to ESG considerations in response to
the growing interest among our customer base.
We see significant growth potential through
capacity-driven initiatives, as demonstrated by
our expansions into new regions like Côte d’Ivoire,
Germany, and the UK since 2019. These strategic
moves reflect our ambitious plans for long-term
growth and have unlocked numerous opportunities
to complement and add value to our core business
of producing cocoa-derived food ingredients.
A prime example of this is our integration into
industrial chocolate production. By utilising internally
produced cocoa-derived food ingredients from
Côte d’Ivoire to manufacture industrial chocolate at
our German production facility, we have established
a fully integrated internal supply chain and achieved
valuable cost efficiencies. Moreover, our presence
and production facilities in Europe and the UK enable
us to label our products as “Made in Europe” and
“Made in the UK,” enhancing our ability to penetrate
markets with strong demand for European and UK-
made chocolate.
Together, these strategies empower us to not only
meet the requirements of our key customers but also
to address their expectations across four core areas:
building a global supply chain presence, enhancing
cost-effectiveness, increasing ESG considerations,
and embracing greater sustainability practices.
Navigating The Surge In
Historic Cocoa Bean Prices
In FY2024, cocoa bean prices surged to
unprecedented levels, driven by a global deficit
resulting from adverse weather conditions and
widespread diseases in origin countries. In response,
we have implemented several strategic measures
to manage the increased costs and ensure the
sustainability of our operations.
To mitigate the impact of rising prices, we have
adjusted the pricing of cocoa products in the
market. At the same time, we are actively managing
our collection processes to ensure timely payments
from customers. Our vigilant approach also involves
closely monitoring supply dynamics in cocoa
origin countries to ensure a steady influx of cocoa
beans to support our production lines. Furthermore,  
we maintain proactive communication with our
banking partners to secure sufficient trade and
working capital lines.
Amidst prevailing market uncertainties, our
unwavering commitment remains focused on
maintaining product quality and navigating these
challenges with resilience.
Prepared To Mitigate Recession
Impacts And Rising Costs
High interest rates and inflation are beginning
to impact household spending power. While we
anticipate stable demand for cocoa and chocolate
products in the short term, we remain vigilant about
potentialchallenges, particularlythe risks associated
with a global economic recession. Although
chocolate is generally considered recession-
resistant, a decline in consumer purchasing power
could still affect demand.
To address these challenges, we are focused on our
core strategies, including maximising the utilisation
of our cocoa grinding facilities, optimising cost
efficiency, and staying agile in response to changes
in the cocoa landscape. We also prioritise strategic,
forward-thinking planning while upholding ethical
and responsible business practices.
We continue to monitor and manage operating costs
and potential risks to ensure our competitiveness
while strengthening stability and efficiency across
our supply chain. These efforts will be crucial in
navigating the uncertain economic future. With
these fundamentals in place, we remain committed
to our three core growth strategies—expansion,
sustainability, and innovation—which will enable us
to maximise cost efficiencies and capture a greater
share of the global cocoa-derived ingredients and
chocolate market.

In FY2024, the
Group recorded an
unprecedented revenue
of RM10.4 billion, reflecting
a remarkable 96.3%
surge from RM5.3 billion

exceptional growth was
largelyattributedtohigher

FINANCIAL
REVIEW
Driven by robust topline performance and improved margins, the Group’s
profit before tax for FY2024 soared by 269.5% to RM513.8 million, compared
to RM139.1 million in the previous year. This remarkable achievement
came despite a 94.1% increase in finance costs, which rose to RM282.5
million from RM145.5 million in FY2023, primarily due to higher borrowings
to support working capital needs amid rising cocoa bean prices. In line
with the strong profit before tax, net profit climbed to RM429.2 million, a
significant increase from RM100.9 million in the previous year.
As of 31st December 2024, the Group’s total assets reached RM10.5 billion,
reflecting a substantial 90.7% increase from RM5.5 billion in the previous
year. This growth was primarily driven by a significant rise in inventory
value, which surged from RM2.5 billion to RM5.5 billion, attributed to
the higher cost of cocoa beans. Additionally, the increase in trade and
other receivables, along with higher derivative financial assets, further
contributed to the expansion in total assets.
As of 31st December 2024, the Group’s total liabilities increased to
RM8.4 billion, marking a 123.2% rise from RM3.7 billion in the previous year.
This increase was primarily driven by higher loans and borrowings to
support growing working capital needs amid rising cocoa bean prices,
coupled with a significant rise in trade and other payables.
Our retained earnings grew from RM1.2 billion in the previous year to
RM1.6 billion in FY2024, underscoring our sustained profitability.
Additionally, the Group’s total shareholders’ equity rose to RM2.1 billion,
a 21.2% increase from RM1.8 billion in the prior year, primarily driven by
higher retained earnings. However, due to the increased need for loans
and borrowings in response to prevailing market conditions, our net
gearing ratio inevitably rose to 1.89 times as of 31st December 2024.
Looking ahead, the Group remains dedicated to closely monitoring
global developments and adapting our growth and pricing strategies
to safeguard long-term sustainability. We will continue to focus on
managing supplier and customer risks while maintaining adequate
working capital to navigate growing market uncertainties.
